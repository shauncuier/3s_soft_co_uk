A suppressed listing is one Amazon has pulled out of search results. The product page still exists, the stock is still there, and you can still reach it by its web address, but shoppers who search for it will not find it. Sellers usually notice when sales on one product stop without warning. This article covers where to look, the causes we see most often when we clean up catalogues, and how to fix each one. Where there is a number, it comes from Amazon's own pages.

## Where do you find suppressed listings in Seller Central?

Go to **Inventory, then Manage All Inventory**, and use the **Search Suppressed and Inactive Listings** tab, or the Suppressed filter. Amazon's listing-errors guide on [sell.amazon.com](https://sell.amazon.com/blog/amazon-listing-errors) describes the same path and the "Incomplete listings" view within it, and Amazon's UK troubleshooting page on [sell.amazon.co.uk](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/learn/listing-guide) points to the Suppressed filter in the same place.

Each suppressed product shows a reason. Click it. In most cases Amazon names the exact field or image that caused the suppression, and the fix is to supply what is missing and save. The **Fix Your Products** tool, which Amazon's guide links to directly, groups listings by problem and in attribute conflicts lets you accept Amazon's suggested value in one click.

If nothing is listed, you have no suppressed listings and the drop in sales has another cause.

## Why do main images cause so many suppressions?

Image problems are the largest single cause we see, and they are the easiest to check. Amazon's UK guidance sets out the rules for the main image:

- **A pure white background**, RGB 255, 255, 255, with no text, logos, hands or lifestyle setting. Amazon's scanner is stricter than the eye; a background that looks white but is 250, 250, 250 will fail.
- **At least 1,000 pixels on each side.** Amazon recommends 1,500 pixels or more so that zoom works.
- **The product filling at least 85 per cent of the frame.**
- **A main image that matches what the customer gets.** A single belt in the photograph when the listing is a pack of three, or the reverse, is a suppression.

The fix is to upload a compliant main image and save. The product photography you use for the A+ page or the brand store, with props and lifestyle settings, belongs in the secondary image slots, not the main one. Our [A+ Content image sizes guide](/insights/amazon-a-plus-content-image-sizes-guidelines/) covers those separately.

## What title and text problems get a listing hidden?

The second group is text. Amazon's UK guidance lists these causes:

- **Titles over the limit.** The general limit is 200 characters, and some categories are capped at 80 or 150. Keyword-stuffed titles that read as a list are the usual offenders.
- **Promotional words, special characters and capital letters.** "FREE DELIVERY", "BEST SELLER" and strings of exclamation marks are all flagged.
- **Prohibited claims in bullet points.** Health claims and unsupported superlatives.
- **Competitor brand names** anywhere in the title, bullets, description or back-end search terms.
- **Off-Amazon contact details**: phone numbers, email addresses, web addresses and QR codes.

Rewrite the field in plain language, keep the brand, product type, key attribute and size in the title, and save. If you are unsure which part triggered it, the suppression reason usually names the field.

## Which missing attributes stop a product showing?

The third group is the catalogue itself. Amazon requires certain attributes per product type so the product can be placed in search filters, and when one is missing the listing is suppressed until it is filled. The reason will name the field: colour, size, material, department, item type and so on.

- **A required attribute is empty.** Open the alert, fill in the field it names, save and finish.
- **The product is in the wrong product type.** A belt listed as "Apparel" rather than "Belt" will be asked for attributes that make no sense for it. Amazon's UK guidance says the fix is to delete and recreate the listing under the correct product type, which is why it is worth getting right first time.
- **Safety or compliance information is required.** Products with batteries or anything classed as dangerous goods need the questionnaire completed before they can show.

For a handful of products this is a few minutes each. For hundreds, download the **Listing Quality and Suppressed Listing Report** from Inventory Reports, fill in the missing columns in the spreadsheet, and upload the corrections as a file. This is the part of catalogue work we do most for clients, and it is where variations, attributes and categories get fixed together rather than one ASIN at a time.

## What are the error codes about brands and generic products?

A different kind of suppression stops the listing being created at all. Amazon's listing-errors guide explains the common codes:

- **5461, 5664 and 5665** are brand-name errors: you do not have permission to create pages for that brand, you listed a generic product without "generic" as the brand, or the brand has not been approved. The fixes are to check the brand spelling, link your Brand Registry and selling accounts, or complete the application the error links to.
- **5885, 5886 and 5887** concern generic products and other sellers' pages. Amazon states that 5886 and 5887 cannot be appealed; the answer is to list the product generically under your own listing.

If you are the brand owner and keep hitting these, the usual cause is that your Brand Registry account and your selling account are not linked, which is a settings fix rather than a listing fix.

## How do you stop it happening again?

1. **Check Manage All Inventory weekly** for the Suppressed filter, and treat any entry as urgent. Amazon's UK guidance notes that listings suppressed for seven or more days can lose search ranking.
2. **Fix the main image rules once, for every product**, rather than one at a time when they fail.
3. **Build new listings with the full attribute set** from the start. It takes longer on day one and avoids the suppression on day thirty.
4. **Keep back-end search terms clean.** Competitor brand names in search terms are invisible to you and visible to Amazon.
5. **Use the Listing Quality Dashboard** to see attribute gaps before they become suppressions.
