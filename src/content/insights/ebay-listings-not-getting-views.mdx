Views on eBay are not random. Your listing appears, or does not, because of a sorting system eBay calls Best Match, and eBay publishes a short page saying what it looks at. Most low-view listings we are asked to look at fail on two or three of those factors, and the fixes cost nothing but an hour. This article goes through the factors in eBay's own words and the order we fix them in.

## What does eBay say Best Match actually looks at?

eBay's help page on [optimising listings for Best Match](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/listing-tips/optimising-listings-best-match?id=4166) lists the factors in its default sort order:

- How closely the listing matches the buyer's search terms
- The item's popularity
- The item's price
- Listing quality, including the description and photos
- How complete the listing is
- Listing terms, such as your return policy and handling time
- Your track record as a seller

Notice what is not on that list. Nothing about ending and relisting, nothing about listing at a particular time of day, nothing about a template. The folklore tactics are not in eBay's own description of the system. The first three items are about whether your listing matches what the buyer typed; the rest are about whether eBay trusts the listing and the seller. Low views usually mean a failure on the first group. Views without sales usually mean a failure on the second.

## Is your title written for buyers or for you?

eBay's advice is a title that is accurate, correctly spelled and **no more than 80 characters**. The mistakes we see most often are the opposite of each other.

**The vague title.** "Lovely belt, great condition" matches nobody's search. A buyer types the brand, the item, the size and the colour, and the title has none of them.

**The stuffed title.** "Belt Belts Mens Men Leather Real Genuine Black Brown Tan Designer Gift" is 80 characters of noise. It matches many searches weakly and none well, and it reads as spam to a buyer scanning results.

Write the title the way a buyer would describe the item to a friend: brand, item, key attribute, size, colour. "Ivan Troy Ivo men's Italian leather dress belt, black croc, 36 inch" says what it is. Use the characters you have left for a second genuine attribute, not a synonym.

## Have you filled in the item specifics?

Item specifics are the structured fields under the title: brand, size, colour, material, style and the rest. eBay's [seller centre page on item specifics](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/listings/item-specifics) says that **"completed item specifics are essential for being found on both eBay and external search engines"**, and explains why: buyers filter search results by them, especially on phones, and a listing with the field empty drops out of the filtered results entirely.

eBay sorts specifics into three groups. **Required** ones, such as brand, size and colour in categories like fashion, home and garden, toys and vehicle parts, block the listing from being created or revised until they are filled. **Recommended** ones are the fields buyers filter by; eBay strongly recommends filling as many as apply. **Additional** ones describe features that may help a sale.

The listing that gets no views is very often the one where only the required specifics are filled, so it disappears the moment a buyer taps a filter. Go back through your live listings and complete the recommended fields. For a large catalogue, eBay's bulk editing tools let you do it in a spreadsheet.

## Do your photos meet eBay's rules, and do they sell?

eBay's [picture policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/listing-policies/picture-policy?id=4370) requires at least one photo of at least 500 pixels on the longest side, and the help page on [adding pictures](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/adding-pictures-listings?id=4148) recommends around 1,600 by 1,600 pixels so that zoom works, with a 12 MB limit per file. The Best Match page says to use high-quality photos taken from every angle and to show any flaws. Up to 24 photos are free.

Meeting the minimum is not the same as selling. A phone photo on a carpet meets the policy. What converts is a clean, well-lit shot of the item on a plain background as the first picture, then the angles, the label, the measurements and the flaws. Buyers on eBay are comparing against the listing next to yours; the one with twelve clear photographs wins against the one with two dark ones at the same price.

## Are your price, postage and terms putting buyers off?

eBay lists the item's price, postage, return policy and handling time as factors. In practice they decide whether a buyer who sees your listing clicks it, and eBay's "popularity" factor is partly a measure of that.

- **Check the price against sold listings, not live ones.** The live listings at a high price are the ones not selling.
- **Show the postage cost clearly** and keep it reasonable. A low item price with high postage reads as a trick, and eBay's Best Match page specifically names reasonable postage.
- **State your returns policy and handling time**, and keep the handling time short. A one-day handling time is a promise you have to keep, but it is one of the few terms that is directly in eBay's list.
- **Describe the item accurately**, using eBay's catalogue product details where they exist. The Best Match page recommends this, and it also feeds the item specifics.

## Is your seller track record holding the listing back?

The last factor is you. Late dispatch, cases closed without seller resolution and low detailed seller ratings pull every listing on the account down, however good the listing is. If your views dropped across the whole account at once rather than on one item, look at your seller dashboard before you touch a single listing. The fix there is operational, not editorial: dispatch on time, answer messages, and resolve returns before the buyer escalates.

## What order should you fix things in?

1. **Rewrite titles** on your top twenty listings by stock value: brand, item, attribute, size, colour, under 80 characters.
2. **Complete the recommended item specifics** on the same listings.
3. **Replace the first photo** with a clean, well-lit shot of at least 1,600 pixels, then add angles and flaws.
4. **Check price against sold listings**, show postage, and shorten handling time.
5. **Only then consider promoting** the listings that now convert.

Give the changes a fortnight. If views on the fixed listings rise and the untouched ones stay flat, roll the same changes out across the catalogue.
