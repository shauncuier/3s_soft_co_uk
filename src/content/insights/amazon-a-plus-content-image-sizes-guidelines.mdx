If you have searched for A+ Content image sizes you will have found a dozen tables that disagree with each other. Some are years out of date, some mix up Basic and Premium, and some list Brand Story modules as if they were A+ modules. This page is the one we use when we build A+ pages for clients, kept to what Amazon's own pages say and what the A+ Content Manager asks for on each slot.

A note on sources. Amazon publishes the general rules on its public [A+ Content guide](https://sell.amazon.com/blog/a-plus-content-design-guide). The exact pixel size for each module is shown on the image slot itself inside A+ Content Manager, behind the Seller Central login, and Amazon changes those sizes from time to time. The sizes below are what the slots show in October 2026. If the label on a slot says something different when you upload, the slot wins.

## What is A+ Content, and who can use it?

A+ Content replaces the plain text product description on an Amazon listing with designed modules: images, feature callouts, comparison charts and short blocks of copy. Amazon says you need a Professional selling plan and enrolment in Amazon Brand Registry, which in turn needs a registered trade mark. The same guide confirms that **Basic A+ Content allows up to five modules per product** and **Premium A+ Content allows up to seven**, with Premium adding full-width images, video and interactive modules.

Brand Story is a separate thing. It is the carousel that appears under the heading "From the brand" and it shows on every listing your brand owns, so you only build it once. You do not need A+ Content to use Brand Story, and you do not need Brand Story to use A+ Content, but the two work best together. Our [Ivan Troy case study](/case-studies/ivan-troy-amazon-brand-story/) shows both on a live Amazon.com listing.

## What file size and format does Amazon accept?

Amazon's guide is clear on one number: **images should be no larger than 2 MB**. In practice we export every A+ image as a JPEG at around 80 per cent quality, or as a PNG when the image has flat colour or a logo that JPEG compression would smudge. Keep the colour profile in sRGB. Images in CMYK, which print designers sometimes supply, upload fine and then look dull or shifted on the page.

Amazon also says to avoid low-resolution or animated images. GIF animation does not play in A+ modules, so there is no point supplying it.

## What size is each Basic A+ module image?

These are the pixel sizes shown on each image slot in A+ Content Manager for the Standard (Basic) modules. Width comes first.

| Module | Image size (px) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Standard Company Logo | 600 × 180 | Logo only, centred |
| Standard Image Header with Text | 970 × 600 | The usual hero module |
| Standard Image & Dark Text Overlay | 970 × 300 | Text box sits on the image |
| Standard Image & Light Text Overlay | 970 × 300 | As above, light text |
| Standard Single Image & Sidebar | 300 × 400 main, 350 × 175 sidebar | Two images |
| Standard Single Image & Highlights | 300 × 300 | Image plus three highlight blocks |
| Standard Single Image & Specs Detail | 300 × 300 | Image plus spec text |
| Standard Single Left Image | 300 × 300 | Text to the right |
| Standard Single Right Image | 300 × 300 | Text to the left |
| Standard Three Images & Text | 300 × 300 each | Three columns |
| Standard Four Image & Text | 220 × 220 each | Four columns |
| Standard Four Image/Text Quadrant | 135 × 135 each | Four small tiles |
| Standard Multiple Image Module A | 300 × 300 each | Up to four, with thumbnails |
| Standard Comparison Chart | 150 × 300 per column | Up to six products |

Three modules take no image at all: Standard Text, Standard Product Description Text and Standard Technical Specifications. They are useful for the size guide or care instructions that buyers search for on the page, and they cost nothing in image preparation.

## What size are the Brand Story images?

Brand Story has its own module set with its own sizes. The background image is wide and short, and Amazon asks for a separate mobile version because the desktop image would be cropped badly on a phone.

| Brand Story module | Image size (px) |
|---|---|
| Brand Story background (desktop) | 1464 × 625 |
| Brand Story background (mobile) | 463 × 625 |
| Brand ASIN & Store Showcase | 362 × 453 |
| Brand Focus Image | 362 × 453 |
| Brand Logo & Description | 315 × 145 logo |
| Brand Q&A | No image |

The showcase and focus cards are portrait, which catches people out when the only brand photography is landscape. Plan a portrait crop for each card before the shoot if you can.

## How are Premium A+ images different?

Premium modules are wider. Full-width Premium images are 1464 pixels across rather than 970, and the manager asks for a separate mobile image on most of them, so each module is two exports rather than one. Premium also adds video, which Amazon says plays at 720p, and interactive hotspot modules. Eligibility for Premium is set by Amazon and shown inside A+ Content Manager; Amazon's public guide does not list the criteria, so do not trust a blog that claims to.

## What are the content rules that get pages rejected?

Image size is the easy part. Most rejections we see are about what the page says. Amazon's guide lists what A+ Content must not include:

- Claims such as "best-selling", "top-rated" or "number one" in text or images
- Warranty or guarantee information in text fields
- Pricing, promotions, shipping details, QR codes, hyperlinks, directions to other websites or contact details
- Any reference to, or comparison with, competitors
- Low-resolution or animated images

Awards or endorsements have to name the certifying body and the year. And you must hold the rights to every image, video and line of text you upload, which rules out supplier photography you have not been licensed to use.

Amazon's guide also recommends keeping text out of images so that buyers on phones can read it. We follow a softer version of that rule: a headline and short labels inside the image are fine, paragraphs are not.

## How do we prepare A+ images so they pass first time?

The process we use on client pages, including the [Ivan Troy belt range](/case-studies/ivan-troy-amazon-brand-story/), is simple:

1. **Choose the modules first, then shoot or crop for them.** Deciding the layout after the photography is how you end up stretching a landscape shot into a portrait card.
2. **Build every image at exactly the slot size.** No bigger, no smaller. Larger files get downscaled and lose crispness; smaller ones are stretched and go soft.
3. **Export under 2 MB, in sRGB, as JPEG or PNG.** Check the file size before upload rather than after a rejection.
4. **Write alt text for every image.** The slot has a field for it. It helps screen reader users and it is indexed.
5. **Proofread the text fields against the content rules.** Price, delivery, "best" claims and contact details are the usual culprits.
6. **Preview on mobile inside the manager.** Most Amazon shoppers are on a phone. If the headline in your hero image is unreadable at that size, change the image, not the headline.
